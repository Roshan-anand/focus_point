# Focus Point Photography

Design direction for review. The homepage and story spread appear together in [the review board](portfolio-review.jpg). Both use generated stand-in photography.

## Browse the work

The opening photograph places the couple on the right and leaves the darker foliage on the left for “Just before the shutter.” The next line places the studio in Sullia. **Browse all work** opens the portfolio index with every category visible. Keep **Enquire** available in the masthead and at the end of each story.

The ivory index gives **Weddings & engagements**, **Modelling** and **Albums** equal prominence. Each category leads to individual shoots or finished albums. Use the real shoot title and a representative image for each entry.

## Tell one story through the edit

In the sample spread, the wide frame establishes the path. A closer frame picks out the reaching hands. The largest photograph catches her glance back. A visitor can read that sequence in one glance, then linger over the chosen frame.

Use this composition when the studio has adjacent frames from one shoot. For a modelling session, the sequence might follow a change of pose. For an album, it might move from the selected photograph to its place on a spread and then to the bound book. Write captions from what is visible. Add the photographer’s own account when available.

## Visual system

- Use the supplied script logo on charcoal. Set headings in Baskervville Regular and navigation, captions, actions and body copy in Inter. These type choices guide the build. The raster comps approximate the lettering.
- Use charcoal `#121417` for the masthead and dark gallery surrounds, ivory `#F4F1EA` for album pages, deep bark `#2E2A24` for text, and moss `#5A6B4F` for the primary action. Reserve temple gold `#C9A45C` for a thin accent on charcoal.
- Keep photography rectangular, with natural skin tones and enough space around faces and gestures. Use a wide hero and a two-column story spread on desktop. Cap the content at 1440 px.
- On phones, put the headline and browse action on charcoal below a separately cropped photograph. Stack the category links. Read the story top to bottom in capture order, with the selected frame last.
- Use native scrolling and still pictures. Keep the sequence readable with a keyboard and with reduced motion enabled.

## Photography for the first real story

Choose one shoot with a horizontal establishing image, a detail and the selected portrait. Ask the photographer what caught their eye in the final frame. That observation can replace the sample caption.
